How to Find Your Google Play App Signing SHA-256 Fingerprint (2026)

Published December 2024 • Updated for 2026 Console Standards • 15 min read • By Senior Android QA Lead
⚡ Quick Answer: Finding Your App Signing SHA-256 Fingerprint

To find your production SHA-256 certificate fingerprint: open Google Play Console > select your app > in the left menu scroll to Release > Setup > App integrity > select the App signing tab. Locate the section titled App signing key certificate and copy the 64-character hex string in the SHA-256 certificate fingerprint row.

Critical Warning: Do not copy the "Upload key certificate" fingerprint for production services like assetlinks.json or Firebase Auth. When Play App Signing is enabled, Google re-signs your app with a cloud signing key. Using the upload key fingerprint causes Digital Asset Links verification and Google Sign-In to fail!

1. Two Keys, Two Fingerprints: Understanding the Difference

Since August 2021, all new applications published to Google Play must use Android App Bundles (AAB) and Google Play App Signing. This architecture introduces two separate cryptographic keys:

1. Your Upload Key (Local)

The keystore file (.jks or .keystore) generated on your computer in Android Studio. Used exclusively to sign your AAB bundle before uploading it to Google Play Console.

Used only between you and Google Console.

2. App Signing Key (Google Cloud)

The key securely managed on Google's cloud infrastructure. Google strips your upload signature and re-signs the final APKs distributed to users and testers with this key.

REQUIRED for assetlinks.json and Firebase.

2. Step-by-Step: Locating the SHA-256 Fingerprint in Play Console

1

Open Google Play Console

Log in at play.google.com/console and select your application.

2

Navigate to App Integrity

In the left navigation sidebar, scroll down to the Release section, expand Setup, and click App integrity.

3

Select the App Signing Tab

Click on the App signing tab at the top of the App integrity dashboard.

4

Copy the SHA-256 Certificate Fingerprint

Under App signing key certificate, find the row labeled SHA-256 certificate fingerprint. Click the copy icon to copy the fingerprint.

3. Where and How to Configure Your SHA-256 Fingerprint

The SHA-256 fingerprint is required in two critical locations:

A. Digital Asset Links (assetlinks.json)

If your app uses Android App Links or is built as a Trusted Web Activity (TWA), you must publish an assetlinks.json file at https://yourdomain.com/.well-known/assetlinks.json: 

[{
  "relation": ["delegate_permission/common.handle_all_urls"],
  "target": {
    "namespace": "android_app",
    "package_name": "com.yourcompany.appname",
    "sha256_cert_fingerprints": [
      "14:6D:E9:83:C5:73:06:50:D8:EE:B9:95:2F:34:FC:64:16:A0:83:42:E6:1D:BE:A8:8A:04:96:B2:3F:CF:44:E5"
    ]
  }
}]
Digital Asset Links Server Requirements
  • • Must be served over HTTPS with a valid SSL certificate.
  • • HTTP Content-Type header must be application/json.
  • • Must return HTTP Status 200 OK without any URL redirects (301 or 302).
  • • Must be accessible by Google's verification crawler without firewalls or CAPTCHAs.

4. Verifying Your assetlinks.json Deployment via CLI

Test your live assetlinks deployment using curl in your terminal: 

curl -I https://yourdomain.com/.well-known/assetlinks.json

Confirm that HTTP response is 200 OK and content-type: application/json.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Google Sign-In return Error 10 or Error 12500?

Developer Error 10 or 12500 occurs when your Firebase Console or Google Cloud OAuth credentials contain your local upload keystore fingerprint instead of the Google Play App Signing key fingerprint. Add both fingerprints in Firebase Console > Project Settings > Your Android App.

Can Google change my App Signing Key once generated?

No. Once generated, Google keeps the app signing key permanent to ensure seamless updates for your existing users. You can request a reset of your local upload key if lost, but the cloud app signing key remains constant.
